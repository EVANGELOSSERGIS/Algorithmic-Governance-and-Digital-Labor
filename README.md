# Algorithmic Governance & Platform Dynamics Framework

## Overview
This repository outlines a theoretical and applied framework for understanding how artificial intelligence, algorithms, and digital platforms shape socio-cultural production. Based on my ongoing advanced sociological research, it bridges critical social theory with practical AI alignment, focusing on data ethics, algorithmic bias, and digital labor.

## Core Research Pillars

### 1. Algorithmic Bias & The Attention Economy
* **Echo Chambers & Filter Bubbles:** Analyzing how recommendation algorithms prioritize engagement metrics, inadvertently reinforcing ideological segregation and algorithmic biases.
* **Algorithmic Curation:** Evaluating how platform algorithms dictate cultural trends, moving away from organic socialization to automated, metric-driven visibility.

### 2. Digital Labor & The "Prosumer"
* **Data Extraction & Surplus Value:** Examining the digital factory model where users and creators function as prosumers (producers-consumers), providing free digital labor and behavioral metadata.
* **Algorithmic Self-Discipline:** Understanding how creative output is structurally modified to meet algorithmic demands, forcing users into a state of internalized algorithmic surveillance.

### 3. Socio-Technical Networks & Human-AI Interaction
* **Algorithms as Non-Human Actants:** Utilizing Actor-Network Theory to treat algorithms not merely as tools, but as active mediators that translate and alter human agency.
* **The Cyborg Identity:** Exploring the bio-technological merger where digital personas and algorithmic metrics become an inseparable part of human identity.

### 4. Big Tech Monopolies & Digital Governance
* **Chokepoint Capitalism:** Investigating the monopolistic control of data and digital infrastructure by tech giants, leading to platform illiberalism and digital authoritarianism.
* **The Vectoralist Class:** Analyzing how modern power structures rely on the ownership of digital vectors and data pipelines, fundamentally changing the political economy of the internet.

## Application to Generative AI & Alignment
By comprehensively mapping the political economy of digital platforms, this framework is utilized to:
1. Identify and mitigate systemic biases in AI training datasets.
2. Ensure structural accuracy and ethical compliance in Generative AI models.
3. Formulate robust policy guidelines that protect human agency in highly automated systems.
